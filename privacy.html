Lidkeys · Privacy policy

What we collect, and why.

Lidkeys is a Mac app made by Lidworks, an independent developer. This page covers the app, this website, the waitlist and tester invites, and buying a Lidkeys Pro license (nothing is for sale while Lidkeys is in its free beta).

The short version: the app has no account, no analytics, no ads and no crash reporting. When you buy, our payment partner Creem handles your payment details; we only get what we need to issue your license key. We never sell your data.

Last updated 24 November 2026

The app

It works on your Mac.

  • Accessibility permission. Lidkeys reads the menus of the app in front and presses the menu item you tap, and, for text keys, inserts the text into the field you’re typing in. Menu names and what you do with them stay on your Mac.
  • Settings, trial and license are stored on your Mac (in its preferences, its Application Support folder and your Keychain). Nothing is synced to us.
  • Usage counts. Lidkeys counts how often you press things on the bar, to keep your most-used keys within reach and to show the Stats page in Settings. For the Stats page it keeps totals, one count per day for the last 35 days, and a count for each key you use, together with the app the key belongs to (the app’s bundle identifier, such as com.apple.Notes), that key’s icon and its menu command name. To keep your most-used keys within reach it also keeps a count for each key in each app. A menu command’s name can include a file name (for example, an item under Open Recent); it stays on your Mac with the rest. Apart from the date and time counting started and the moments you reached a milestone or earned a badge (and, if your trial ends, when that happened), Lidkeys does not record when you pressed something; it also does not record where the pointer was, window titles, or anything you type. When you update from an earlier version, the key counts it already kept are added to these totals once. All of this is stored only on your Mac, in the app’s preferences, and is never sent to us or to anyone else. Reset Stats in Settings clears everything on the Stats page at any time; the per-key counts Lidkeys uses to order your keys stay until you remove the app’s preferences. A stats card is created on your Mac only when you save, copy, share or drag one. It contains numbers, key icons and badges, with no app names, menu command names, file names or anything you typed, and it goes only where you put it.
  • Network requests. The app only goes online in these cases:
    • During the free beta there is nothing to buy or activate, so the app makes no license requests.
    • Activating Pro with a key from Gumroad or Polar (paid versions only): the app sends that key and our product ID to the store that sold it, to check it is valid and not refunded. Keys bought on this website are checked on your Mac, with no request at all.
    • The optional AI quota pill: it reads usage from Claude Code or Codex on your Mac. If you give it a DeepSeek API key, it sends that key to DeepSeek (api.deepseek.com) to read your balance. The key stays on your Mac otherwise.
    • Links such as "Buy" or "Help" open in your web browser.
  • Commands you use. To offer Again, to grow keys for commands you pick often, and to show command captions, the app notes which menu commands you run from the bar or choose from the menu bar with the mouse: the command’s name, its place in the menu, its keyboard shortcut if it has one, how often you use it and on which day. For apps where a key has grown on its own, it also notes on which of the last 14 days you used that app, so an unused grown key can retire by itself. It learns this from the system’s accessibility notifications about menus. To tell where one round of commands ends and the next begins, Again also remembers, in memory only, that you clicked in that app, never where or on what. These features never look at your keyboard, so commands you run with keyboard shortcuts are not seen at all. Menu items that can contain your own content (open windows, recent files, history and bookmarks) are never recorded. All of this stays on your Mac, and you can turn it off or clear it in Settings › Keys. Clearing it removes the counts; keys that grew on their own, and the ones you swiped away, are cleared separately with Reset Grown Keys in the same place.
  • Custom keys. If you make a key that inserts text, opens a link or file, or runs several menu commands, Lidkeys keeps that text, link, file location or list of command names in its preferences on this Mac, unencrypted. Don’t store passwords in it. If you export your keys, the exported file contains them too. When you press a text key, Lidkeys inserts the text into the field you’re typing in using macOS Accessibility. It does not use or read your clipboard. None of this leaves your Mac.
  • Things you park on the Shelf. For files, the app remembers only where each file is. It never copies, moves, opens or uploads them; the small preview on the Shelf comes from macOS and is kept in memory only. Text and links you park are kept on your Mac. A picture dragged from a web page has no file to point to, so the app saves one copy in its own folder on your Mac while it sits on the Shelf. When you use Quick Look on parked text, a temporary text file is written and deleted as soon as the preview closes. To gray out items whose original file was moved to the Trash or a disk was ejected, the app checks whether the file still exists (without opening it), and only after you have used your Mac. All of it is removed when you take the item off the Shelf, and nothing is kept after you quit unless you have Pro and leave "Keep Shelf items after quitting" on. If your trial ends, what was kept stays on this Mac, hidden, until you buy Pro (it comes back) or choose Remove All in Settings. The app writes to the clipboard only when you choose Copy on a Shelf item.
  • Downloads, if you turn it on. If you turn on Downloads, Lidkeys lists the files in your Downloads folder to show the newest ones on the bar, and makes thumbnails of the newest few on your Mac. It never opens or reads the files themselves, and nothing leaves your Mac. It reads only names, dates added and sizes, and is off until you switch it on; macOS asks for your permission at that moment. A new file's thumbnail shows for 10 minutes, then the bar goes back to a plain icon. The app's troubleshooting log notes only how many files arrived, never their names.
  • Screenshots, if you turn it on. If you turn on Screenshots, Lidkeys watches the folder where macOS saves your screenshots (usually Desktop), checks which files are screenshots, and makes thumbnails on your Mac. While it is on, the macOS floating thumbnail is switched off so screenshots are saved right away; it is switched back when you turn Screenshots off or quit, and if you turn the floating thumbnail back on yourself, Lidkeys leaves it on. Nothing leaves your Mac. It reads only names, dates added, sizes and the marks macOS puts on screenshots (where on the screen it was taken and what kind it is). The bar sits on your screen, so a later screenshot, recording or screen share can include it; that is why a new screenshot's thumbnail shows on the bar for only 60 seconds before the bar goes back to a plain icon. The troubleshooting log notes only how many screenshots arrived and how long they took, never their names.
  • Word Count, if you turn it on. If you turn on Word Count, Lidkeys reads the text in the field you are typing in to count it. The text is counted in memory on your Mac and discarded; it is never saved, logged or sent anywhere. Password fields are never read.
  • Input Monitoring, if you allow it. Emoji while typing and Word Count need to notice that you are typing, so Lidkeys asks for the macOS Input Monitoring permission the first time you go to type in a chat app. It only notices that a key was pressed and which key position it was, to decide whether to show emoji; it never records, saves or sends what you type.
  • Selected text. Lidkeys reads the text you have selected only at the moment you click Look Up, Search or Speak on the bar, uses it for that one action and then discards it.
  • Clipboard history, if you turn it on. Clipboard history is off by default. If you turn it on, it keeps your recent copied text in memory only, so it is gone when you quit, and it skips anything a password manager marks as concealed.
  • Emoji and stickers. Stickers you add are stored on this Mac. Lidkeys reads only the folders you pick yourself in the macOS folder window, and a folder you choose to follow is remembered only on this Mac. When you slice a screenshot of a sticker panel, the screenshot is cut up in memory and never saved; the stickers that come out of it are stored on this Mac with a short fingerprint used to spot duplicates. When you send a sticker, a temporary copy may be written to the system’s temporary folder and is cleared after 7 days. Lidkeys counts, per chat app, which emoji and stickers you sent from the bar and how often, only on this Mac.
  • Web page address. To tell whether you are typing in a web version of a chat app, Lidkeys reads the address of the page you are typing in, compares it with its list of chat sites, and does not keep it.
  • Playback. Lidkeys sends “jump to” or “change speed” to the app that is playing only when you drag the progress bar or tap the speed control yourself.
  • Key history. For each key on the bar, Lidkeys keeps on this Mac how many days it has been shown and whether you have pressed it, so keys you never use can move out of the way.
  • Snap fan. When you pick a layout, Lidkeys moves only the window you chose, using the Accessibility permission; it doesn’t read window titles or contents.
  • Watch (Pro). When you switch away from an app, Lidkeys checks how busy that app and its helper processes are (processor time and bytes written to disk). Only if it’s busy does it look for a progress bar in that app’s windows, using the Accessibility permission you already gave it; it reads only the bar’s number, never window titles, text or file names. To know which processes belong to that app, it looks at the parent of each process running under your account, but not their names. While it’s watching (or when you choose Notify When Done), it repeats this once a second. If you ask it to put the Mac to sleep when the job is done, it first checks which apps are asking the Mac to stay awake (only the kind of request and which process made it, not the request’s description). All of this happens on this Mac and nothing is sent anywhere. The only thing kept is a line in the app’s local troubleshooting log on this Mac, noting which app was watched and its progress (no window titles, text or file names).
  • Timer. The timer’s state (how long it runs and when it ends) is kept in the app’s preferences on your Mac, so it survives a restart. Nothing else is recorded.
  • Keep Awake. If you use the Keep Awake switch, the app asks macOS to keep your display on for the time you chose and remembers that length for next time; macOS ends this automatically if the app quits. Nothing leaves your Mac.
  • Sounds and little touches. The app makes its sounds itself (it writes the few short sound files it plays into its own cache folder on your Mac); it never uses the microphone and never records. It only checks whether another app is using the microphone, and goes quiet while one is. The moon on the corner ball is worked out on your Mac from the date and your time zone; the app never asks for your location. Patina (keys you press a lot slowly take on a polish) is based only on the press counts already kept on this Mac, and you can start it over in Settings. After two minutes with no input, the corner ball can glow slowly like an old MacBook’s sleep light; to decide that, the app reads how long the Mac has been idle and whether any app is keeping the display awake. These are single numbers that are used once and never stored. For the dust on the corner ball, it also reads when your Mac last restarted and remembers that time on this Mac. On a few winter days, and every day from December 20 to January 2, a little snow settles on the bar. Which days is worked out only from the date and your Mac’s time zone setting (so winter matches your hemisphere): it is not a weather report, and Lidkeys never looks up your location or the weather. How much snow sits on each part of the bar is saved on your Mac so it stays put after a restart; nothing about it is sent anywhere, and it is deleted once the snow melts.
Buying Lidkeys Pro

Creem takes the payment. We get the order.

  • Creem (creem.io) is our merchant of record: it runs the checkout, charges your card or other payment method, collects sales tax or VAT, and sends your receipt. It collects your name, email, billing country and payment details under its own privacy policy. We never see your full card number.
  • What we receive from Creem: the order ID, your email address, your country, the product and the amount paid.
  • What we do with it: your license key is made from your email address and the order date, and is shown to you right after checkout. Our license server, which runs on Cloudflare, keeps a record of each order ID and the key issued for it, so you can recover a lost key with your order ID and email, and so a refunded order stops getting keys. We also use your email to answer you and, if needed, to handle a refund.
  • We don't send newsletters or marketing email unless you ask us to.
This website

No cookies, no trackers.

  • The website is hosted on GitHub Pages. We use no cookies, analytics or advertising trackers. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security, as described in GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Public downloads are closed for now. People testing Lidkeys get a personal invite link; that download comes from our small server on Cloudflare (see below).
Waitlist and tester invites

Your email, only if you leave it.

  • Leaving your email is optional. If you type your email into the “Coming soon” box, we keep that email address, the time you left it and your browser’s language setting. We use it only to tell you when downloads open — one email, no newsletters — and we never give it, sell it or share it with anyone. We don’t store your IP address with it.
  • Getting it deleted: write to lidworks@proton.me from that address (or tell us the address) and we’ll delete it. Once downloads open and we’ve written to you, we delete the list.
  • Tester invite links. An invite link carries a code. When you open it, the page asks our server whether the code is still good; when you download, the server counts the download against that code and notes the time and the version. It doesn’t store your IP address or anything else about you. We know who a code was given to only because we gave it to them.
  • Repeated requests from the same connection are slowed down to stop abuse. Cloudflare counts them in memory for about a minute and keeps nothing.
  • Both run on Cloudflare Workers; the email list and invite records are stored in Cloudflare’s key-value storage.
Who else handles data

Only the services we need.

  • Creem: payments, tax, receipts and refunds.
  • Cloudflare: runs our small server for the waitlist and tester downloads (and, once a paid version exists, the license server).
  • GitHub: hosts this website.
  • Proton: our support email.

We don't sell or rent personal data, and we don't share it with anyone else unless the law requires it.

Your choices

Ask, and we'll act on it.

  • How long we keep it: order records are kept as long as they are needed for your license, refunds and tax records. Support emails are kept while we're helping you.
  • See or delete your data: email us and we'll tell you what we hold about you, correct it, or delete it. If we must keep an order record for tax law, we'll keep only that. Deleting your order record means we can no longer recover your key for you.
  • Children: Lidkeys is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data.
  • Changes: if this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top.
